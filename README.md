## INTRODUCTION 

Public transport in Krakow is one of the most efficient and affordable systems in Poland. Managed mainly by MPK (Miejskie Przedsiębiorstwo Komunikacyjne) and supervised by ZTP Krakow, the network covers almost every part of the city. With over 24 tram lines and more than 180 bus routes, it connects residential areas, universities, and tourist attractions such as the Main Square, Wawel Castle, and Kazimierz district. The system plays a crucial role in the everyday life of citizens.

The city’s tram and bus lines are modern, punctual, and environmentally friendly. Most trams and buses are equipped with air conditioning, ticket machines, and electronic boards showing real-time information. Tickets can be purchased for specific time durations — such as 20, 50, or 90 minutes — and are valid across all public transport types. There are also daily, weekend, and monthly passes for regular passengers. Traveling without a valid ticket can result in a fine of around 240 PLN.

Krakow also promotes sustainable mobility through digitalization and green initiatives. The city supports bike-sharing systems like Wavelo and electric scooters that complement the main transport network. Digital applications such as JakDojade or MPK Mobile allow passengers to check schedules, buy e-tickets, and plan routes efficiently. This integration of technology and ecology shows Krakow’s commitment to becoming a modern, eco-friendly European city.
